# CoTo — Come Together, But Not Right Now

**arXiv:** 2506.05713 (Jun 2025)
**Venue:** ICLR 2026 (OpenReview Zha2m39ZoM)

## Abstract

CoTo is a *training-time intervention* for LoRA: stochastically deactivate a subset of adapters early in training, gradually re-enable them, and the resulting endpoint LoRAs exhibit improved Linear Mode Connectivity (LMC) — interpolating between two trained adapters keeps loss low. The paper evaluates this through standard interpolation-barrier tests on the *endpoint* pair.

## Why this matters for the LoRA-LMC thesis

CoTo is the **single biggest novelty threat** in the corpus. It is the only paper that combines "LoRA" + "LMC" in 2025–2026, so it will be on every reviewer's mind when reading the thesis introduction.

## Differentiator (lead the introduction with this)

CoTo *promotes* LMC via a training method, evaluated on the endpoint pair via barrier tests. The thesis *measures* whether vanilla LoRAs already exhibit within-task collapse under the GL(r)-quotient, with:

- a gauge fix (CoTo has none — they compare raw `(B, A)` interpolation),
- a Region 1/2/3 spectral split (CoTo treats `ΔW` as monolithic),
- a *trajectory* analysis with checkpoints (CoTo uses endpoints only),
- downstream prediction of mergeability/forgetting/generalization from trajectory shape (CoTo predicts merging benefit from a training intervention).

Different question, different evidence, different prediction targets.
