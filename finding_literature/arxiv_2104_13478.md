# Geometric Deep Learning: Grids, Groups, Graphs, Geodesics, and Gauges

**arXiv:** 2104.13478 (v2, May 2021) — proto-book, 156 pages.
Forthcoming as MIT Press textbook (2026).

## Abstract (verbatim-summarized)

Deep learning rests on two pillars: hierarchical representation learning and gradient-descent
optimization. The book argues most successful architectures (CNNs, RNNs, GNNs, Transformers)
implicitly exploit **symmetry and scale separation** — regularities inherited from the
low-dimensional structure of physical reality. Following Klein's *Erlangen Program*, the authors
propose a **unified geometric blueprint** for deep learning: characterize a domain by its symmetry
group, demand equivariance to that group, and derive the appropriate architecture. The blueprint
covers grids (CNNs), sets/graphs (GNNs/Transformers), groups (Group-CNNs), manifolds (intrinsic
CNNs / mesh nets), and gauge fields (gauge-equivariant CNNs).

## Structure (the "5 Gs")

| G | Domain | Symmetry group | Architecture |
|---|--------|----------------|--------------|
| **Grids** | Euclidean lattice $\mathbb{Z}^d$ | translations | CNN |
| **Groups** | Homogeneous space $G/H$ | compact group $G$ (e.g., SE(3), SO(3)) | Group-equivariant CNN, Steerable CNN, Spherical CNN |
| **Graphs** | Sets, networks | permutations $S_n$ | GNN, MPNN, Transformer (graph-no-edges) |
| **Geodesics** | Riemannian manifolds | isometries / diffeomorphisms | Intrinsic / mesh CNN, MoNet, GeoCNN |
| **Gauges** | Tangent-bundle / fiber-bundle data | gauge group (e.g., $SO(d)$ pointwise) | Gauge-equivariant CNN, ICOSAHEDRAL CNN |

Two universal priors run through all five: **symmetry / equivariance** and **scale separation /
locality** (multi-scale composition).

## Why this matters for the weightBench north star

Direct relevance to *weight-aware AI* — not as theory shelf-fill but as the **mathematical engine**
behind every line of work that treats neural-network weights as themselves geometric objects:

1. **Weight space *is* a quotient by symmetry.** Permutation symmetry of hidden units, GL(r)
   gauge symmetry of LoRA factors, scale symmetry of ReLU layers — all are *exactly* the kind
   of group the GDL blueprint says to quotient by before doing geometry. The TRS / LoRA-LMC work
   already does this implicitly (GL(r) gauge fix, Grassmannian trajectory analysis); GDL gives
   the formal vocabulary.
2. **Neural Functional Networks (NFN, DWS, neural-functionals).** Networks whose *input* is
   another network's weights. These must be equivariant to the hidden-unit permutation group
   $S_{n_1} \times \cdots \times S_{n_L}$. GDL chapters on group-equivariant architectures are
   the design manual for any "model that reads its own weights" — i.e., the W2T-style router
   on the north-star list.
3. **Capability introspector.** Predicting task ability from raw weights requires a feature
   extractor that respects the same symmetries the weights have (else two functionally
   identical networks get different predictions). Same blueprint applies.
4. **Self-monitoring fine-tuner.** A model that audits its own spectrum during training is
   reading a *gauge-invariant* signal (eigenvalues are GL invariants); GDL formalizes when
   such invariants are sufficient and when one needs equivariant features instead.

## Anchor concepts to surface in the graph

- **Equivariance** — $\Phi(g \cdot x) = g \cdot \Phi(x)$. Cross-cuts every weight-symmetry note.
- **Invariance** — equivariance to the trivial action; what spectrum-based capability metrics
  produce.
- **Erlangen Program** — Klein's "geometry = study of invariants of a group action." Direct
  parent concept for the LoRA gauge-fix story.
- **Steerable / equivariant features** — features that *transform predictably* under the group
  rather than being thrown away. The right object for weight-space encoders.
- **Scale separation / multi-resolution** — second universal prior; relevant to multi-scale
  spectrum statistics across layers.
- **Group convolution** — generalization of convolution from $\mathbb{Z}^d$ to arbitrary groups;
  template for processing weight tensors.
- **Gauge symmetry** — local frame ambiguity. Direct mathematical analog of LoRA's GL(r)
  ambiguity in $A, B \mapsto AG, G^{-1}B$.
- **Permutation equivariance** — symmetry of GNNs *and* of MLP hidden-unit relabelings; bridge
  between graph-learning and weight-space-learning literatures.
